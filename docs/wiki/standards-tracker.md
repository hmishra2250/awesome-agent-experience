# Standards tracker

In one line: one table of the standards, protocols and conventions that shape agent-facing surfaces, with who stewards each, how settled it is and the latest version seen.

Last reviewed: 2026-11-20. All status and version facts below are as of 2026-11-20, checked against the live specification page, repository or IETF Datatracker entry on that date unless a row says otherwise.

## How to read the table

Status uses four labels. They describe the document, not its adoption or quality.

- **specification**: a published, versioned text that implementers can build against. This includes RFCs and W3C Recommendations, but also project specifications outside any standards body. Where a row says "RFC" or "W3C Recommendation", the text has completed a formal standards process.
- **draft**: the text describes itself as a draft, proposal, beta, preview or release candidate, or it is an IETF Internet-Draft. For Internet-Drafts the row says whether it is a working group document (named `draft-ietf-...`) or an individual submission, which has no standing in the IETF beyond being published.
- **de facto**: a convention used in practice without a formal versioned specification, or with a deliberately minimal one.
- **abandoned**: expired, replaced, merged into another effort or explicitly superseded.

"Latest version seen" gives the version or revision and its publication date as stated by the source. A dash means the source states no version. An Internet-Draft revision expires six months after publication unless it is updated or advances.

Rows are grouped by area: tools and agent protocols; APIs and machine-readable surfaces; instructions and documentation; the agent-readable web; discovery and naming; identity and authorisation; commerce and payments; observability.

## Changes since the curated list's last update (2026-10-12)

These are the movements visible in the table above. Each was checked on 2026-11-20.

- **WebMCP** published a new Draft Community Group Report on 19 November 2026, and a W3C threat model for it appeared as an unofficial draft on 15 November 2026. The W3C Agentic Web Working Group charter is still a draft, with chairs proposed on 11 November 2026.
- **W3C TAG** published its Draft Finding on agentic browsing on 27 October 2026.
- **IETF agentproto** was chartered on 8 October 2026. No document has yet been adopted; the use cases draft reached -07 and an architecture draft -00 appeared.
- **Web Bot Auth** moved to a working group protocol draft (-01, 29 October 2026).
- **Agent authentication at the IETF** moved from draft-klrc-aiagent-auth to the wimse working group document draft-ietf-wimse-aims (-01, 2 November 2026).
- **OAuth** building blocks were revised: Client ID Metadata Document -03 (2 November), ID-JAG -06 (31 October) and OAuth 2.1 -17 (31 October). Identity Chaining sits in the RFC Editor queue.
- **Idempotency-Key** reached -08 (22 October 2026). The **RateLimit** draft (-11) expires on 24 November 2026 unless revised.
- **Agent Skills** recorded its donation to the Agentic AI Foundation, with LF Projects governance, on 4 November 2026.
- **ANS v2** became a Linux Foundation project on 10 November 2026 and has an IETF individual draft (-02).
- **New individual drafts** include DNS-AID (-00, 2 November), PACE (-00, 15 November) and the readiness evaluation draft draft-shah-agent-readiness-evaluation (-00, 17 October), which is a scoring method rather than a protocol and is covered under [Readiness scoring](concepts/readiness-scoring.md).

## What the table does not say

- **Status is not adoption.** A specification with few implementations and a draft with many are both common in this area. Adoption claims mostly come from the projects themselves; see each standard page for the evidence that exists.
- **Status is not quality or effect.** None of the web-facing conventions here (llms.txt, Content Signals, Markdown twins, ARD) has independent evidence that publishing it improves agent task success. See [Evaluating agent experience](concepts/evaluating-agent-experience.md) and [Readiness scoring](concepts/readiness-scoring.md).
- **Individual Internet-Drafts carry no IETF endorsement.** Anyone can publish one. The ones that matter most for tracking are the working group documents (names beginning `draft-ietf-`).
- **Vendor-led specifications can change quickly.** ACP, UCP, AP2, MPP, TAP and Verifiable Intent are all pre-1.0 or vendor-controlled, and no cross-protocol conformance testing is documented.
